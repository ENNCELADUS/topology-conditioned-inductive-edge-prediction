1 · Tokenize-lite and ego-slot imagination

Each unseen endpoint is encoded from its frozen node features and decoded into K soft neighbor slots carrying content h, existence π, multiplicity m, grounding confidence g, and slot adjacency Â. Exact top-50 cosine retrieval supplies a secondary grounding vocabulary; it is not treated as observed topology.

The learned-query, fixed-cardinality set decoder and bipartite set supervision descend from DETR (Carion et al., 2020); EgoStitch changes object queries into imagined neighbor slots and adds existence, multiplicity, and adjacency heads.

2 · Hungarian slot supervision

Predicted slots are matched to training-message neighbors with a compound feature, degree-bucket, and overlap cost. Matched slots receive feature and existence targets; unmatched slots are trained toward absence. Target sampling is keyed by biological node identity, seed, and epoch so the same node receives the same target subset across pairs, directions, and ranks.

The one-to-one assignment is the set-prediction mechanism used by DETR (Carion et al., 2020); here it supervises an unordered ego network rather than a detected-object set.

3 · Sinkhorn alignment and stitched scaffold

A differentiable transport plan Π aligns the two imagined slot sets. The pair scaffold retains both endpoint anchors and both slot sets, then adds star edges, within-ego adjacency, cross-ego alignment, and a closure edge type. The closed-wedge feature t_k=[ΠÂ̊_otherΠᵀ]_{kk} exposes higher-order overlap without importing an observed test graph.

Entropic transport follows Sinkhorn distances (Cuturi, 2013). GOAT (Saad-Eldin et al., 2021) and SLOTAlign (Tang et al., 2023) align observed graphs as the task; EgoStitch uses transport internally to stitch two generated ego networks before edge classification.

4 · Structure-only scaffold encoder

The STE applies three edge-weighted message-passing layers to structural scaffold tokens. Its inputs contain anchor labels, π, m, per-edge-type soft degrees, and closed-wedge mass—never slot content h, grounded identity, or the pair label.

The local-subgraph principle follows SEAL (Zhang and Chen, 2018), while shared-neighbor structure is motivated by NCN (Wang et al., 2023). EgoStitch’s protocol difference is that the encoded subgraph is generated from endpoint features rather than extracted from an observed test graph.

5 · Separate content pathway

Slot content, soft existence, grounding confidence, matched-identity probability, and counterpart-membership remain in c_cont. Keeping this pathway separate from the STE makes a topology-only null measurable instead of allowing content to masquerade as structure.

This graph-to-token interface is related to GraphToken (Perozzi et al., 2024); EgoStitch uses separate generated-structure and slot-content token streams to condition a binary pair classifier.

6 · Centered gated pair conditioning

A from-scratch Siamese pair trunk receives topology and content through separate cross-attention residuals in its last pair block. Each residual is centered by a synchronized active-row mean and scaled by a zero-initialized tanh gate, so a pathway must learn pair-varying evidence rather than a constant offset.

Zero-initialized gated cross-attention follows the fusion pattern of Flamingo (Alayrac et al., 2022); EgoStitch injects generated graph context into a from-scratch symmetric edge classifier rather than images into a frozen language model.

7 · Checkpoint-exact pathway nulls

The same checkpoint publishes full, pair-only f_logit, pair-plus-content, and pair-plus-topology logits. Training drops topology and content branches independently with probability 0.15; evaluation uses exact hard bypasses, and every null preserves p(i,j)=p(j,i).

The branch-training idea follows modality dropout (Abdelaziz et al., 2020); here it supports within-checkpoint mechanism attribution rather than missing-modality robustness alone.

8 · Relational repair objectives

L_ptr grounds eligible slots, L_align rewards double-Hungarian same-identity transport cells, L_div prevents unmatched-slot collapse, and logit-space L_slotadj sharpens adjacency learning. An STE-only auxiliary head predicts log1p common-neighbor count and neighborhood Jaccard for every positive and negative training pair, then is discarded before scoring.

The relational targets use the common-neighbor signal formalized for neural link prediction by NCN (Wang et al., 2023); EgoStitch uses them only as train-side pressure on a generated-scaffold representation.

Training objective

L = L_edge + 0.5 L_real + 0.1 L_ssl + 1.0 L_recon

L_recon = 1.0 L_feat + 0.5 L_exist + 0.25 L_mult + 0.5 L_deg
        + 0.5 L_slotadj + 0.25 L_gate + 0.25 L_ptr
        + 0.5 L_align + 0.1 L_div + 0.25 L_rel

L_edge is the binary queried-pair objective. L_real matches generated and real train-side ego statistics and random-GIN embeddings; L_ssl enforces feature-noise and pool-resampling consistency. Only L_feat, L_exist, L_mult, and L_deg anneal from 1.0 to 0.25 during the edge-active phase; the relational repair terms remain fully weighted.
