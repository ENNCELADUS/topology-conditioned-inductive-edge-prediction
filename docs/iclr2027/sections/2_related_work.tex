\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Interaction prediction from intrinsic protein features.}
The school closest to our task predicts interaction from the two proteins alone,
with no interaction graph as input, and divides into four mechanisms.
\emph{Pooled pair scorers} embed each protein independently with a pLM and
combine the two vectors through concatenation, difference, or a small interaction
head; MAPE-PPI builds per-residue microenvironment codebooks before pooling
\citep{wu2024mapeppi}, and uncertainty-aware and explainability-oriented variants
follow the same template. \emph{Jointly encoded paired-sequence models} instead
attend across both chains at once, giving the encoder access to cross-chain
evidence that concatenation cannot represent
\citep{liu2025plminteract,ullanat2026language}. \emph{Structure- and
interface-based scorers} use resolved or predicted three-dimensional structure of
the queried pair, culminating in proteome-scale screens that combine structural
modeling with sequence signal \citep{zhang2025humanproteome}.
\emph{Retrieval and factorization methods} attack the quadratic cost of
proteome-scale scoring directly: RaftPPI reports that screening the human
proteome with a paired-sequence model costs $148.47$ A100 GPU-days, and reduces
top-$20\%$ retrieval over ${\sim}200$ million pairs to minutes through
residue-level factorization \citep{zhao2026raftppi}. A fifth line addresses
cross-species generalization through orthologue-informed training
\citep{szymborski2024intrepppid}. All five share the property our thesis targets:
each pair is scored independently, and no mechanism in any of them makes the
decision for $(u,v)$ depend on the predicted structure around $u$ and $v$.

Two works partially escape this characterization and deserve explicit
differentiation. DNE learns protein embeddings directly from PPI network
adjacency and evaluates both link prediction and functional-module recovery,
making it genuinely topology-conditioned \citep{yan2024dne}; SCMPPI similarly
injects precomputed network embeddings from a known interactome as an auxiliary
modality \citep{xu2026scmppi}. Both are \emph{transductive}: every queried
protein must already be a node in the training-time graph, so neither can score a
pair in which an endpoint was never observed. Removing exactly that requirement
while keeping topology inside the decision is what our formulation provides.

\paragraph{Benchmarks, leakage, and the shift from pairs to graphs.}
A now-substantial critical literature shows that reported PPI accuracies have
been driven by evaluation protocol rather than by modeling. Random or
identifier-based splits leak the large majority of test cases back into training,
and even sequence-similarity splitting---the field's standard
mitigation---leaves substantial residual leakage \citep{bushuiev2024leakage};
leakage can additionally enter through the \emph{frozen encoder}, since pLMs
pretrained on proteins that later appear in the PPI test set inflate downstream
MCC by $9.14\%$ with the downstream split held identically clean
\citep{szymborski2026flaw}. \citet{bernett2024cracking} show that degree alone
reproduces most published accuracies, and a leakage-free re-evaluation finds deep
sequence models plateauing near $0.65$ accuracy \citep{reim2025plateau}. Negative
sampling is a second structural problem: essentially no verified non-interactions
exist at scale---Negatome lists $2{,}424$ human non-interactions in
total---which forces reliance on sampling heuristics, and the framing of PPI
prediction as positive-unlabeled rather than binary classification predates the
deep learning era \citep{kilic2012pu}. Topology-driven negative sampling
constructs roughly three million hard negatives from $L_3$-path structure and
demonstrates that predictors performing exceptionally well transductively
collapse under genuinely inductive, unseen-node evaluation
\citep{chatterjee2025tppni}. These findings jointly justify the strict
node-disjoint inductive protocol we adopt.

The most direct antecedent of our evaluation is PRING, which reframes PPI
prediction from pairs to graphs and evaluates the \emph{assembled} prediction
with five topology metrics reported together: graph similarity (GS, edge-set Dice
against the true graph), relative density (RD, target $\rightarrow 1$), and MMD
ratios over the degree, clustering-coefficient, and Laplacian-spectral
distributions \citep{zheng2025pring}. PRING's central finding---that no tested
baseline exceeds GS $0.5$ despite strong pairwise scores---is the empirical
statement of the gap our method addresses, and its five-metric reporting
convention is the one we adopt. Relatedly, \citet{lannelongue2024pitfalls} argue
that a single global AUPRC is uninterpretable without the degree composition of
the evaluation set, motivating topology-stratified rather than aggregate
reporting.

\paragraph{Structure-aware link prediction.}
The graph learning literature has established both that topology conditioning is
decisive for link prediction and \emph{how} to do it, provided a graph is given.
SEAL frames link prediction as classification over an extracted enclosing
subgraph \citep{zhang2018seal}; distance encoding and position-aware graph neural
networks supply structural features that plain message passing cannot compute
\citep{li2020distance,you2019pgnn}; and the labeling trick establishes the
theoretical anchor that graph neural networks computing node representations
\emph{independently} cannot distinguish structurally distinct node pairs, so
target-conditioned representations are necessary as well as sufficient
\citep{zhang2021labeling}. Efficiency-oriented successors---Neo-GNN, BUDDY,
NCN/NCNC, and LPFormer---retain explicit neighborhood-overlap modeling while
avoiding per-pair subgraph extraction
\citep{yun2021neognn,chamberlain2023buddy,wang2024ncn,shomer2024lpformer}. Two
evaluation critiques bound what these gains mean: HeaRT shows that standard
benchmarks use trivially easy, non-personalized negatives, and hardening them
substantially reranks methods \citep{li2023heart}, while
\citet{aiyappa2024degree} show that standard positive sampling is degree-biased
enough that a degree-only null model (AUC ${\approx}0.83$) outperforms more than
half of $26$ published methods across $90$ graphs. Every method in this family,
however, reads structure from an observed graph containing the query nodes. That
assumption is exactly what an unseen protein violates, and it is why this
literature---despite being the source of the ``topology matters'' evidence we
rely on---cannot be applied to our task without changing the protocol.

\paragraph{Inductive and cold-start link prediction.}
A smaller literature relaxes the observed-graph assumption. GraphSAGE established
inductive node representations via neighborhood sampling and aggregation
\citep{hamilton2017graphsage}, and Graph2Gauss ranks inductive embeddings from
attributes \citep{bojchevski2018graph2gauss}. GLNN and Cold Brew distill a graph
neural network's structural knowledge into a graph-free student so that inference
needs no neighborhood at all \citep{zhang2022glnn,zheng2022coldbrew}, and NodeDup
augments low-degree nodes for cold-start link prediction \citep{guo2025nodedup}.
The three closest works to our setting each retain an assumption we cannot make.
DEAL predicts links for nodes carrying only attribute information
\citep{hao2020deal}, but always scores the new node against a counterpart whose
structural embedding was trained on the observed graph---never both endpoints
unseen. New-node prediction predicts all links of an isolated node
\citep{zanardini2024newnode}, but explicitly against a fully observed training
graph from which candidate partners are drawn. \citet{chatterjee2023disentangling}
do formalize the fully-mutually-unseen case and quantify the collapse of
structural predictors within it, but their remedy abandons topology conditioning
altogether in favor of a pure attribute-pair scorer---the topology-blind
alternative our thesis argues against. The inductive knowledge graph lineage
achieves entity-inductive reasoning through relational structure
\citep{zhang2020igmc,teru2020grail,zhu2021nbfnet,galkin2024ultra}, but relies on
relation types and an observed relational neighborhood connecting the query pair,
neither of which exists for two proteins with no edges.

\paragraph{Graph structure learning and latent graph inference.}
Graph structure learning (GSL) learns a graph rather than accepting one, and is
therefore the closest mechanism precedent for our scaffold generator. Latent
adjacency inference optimizes a discrete or continuous structure jointly with a
downstream graph neural network---LDS via bilevel optimization over Bernoulli
edge variables \citep{franceschi2019lds}, IDGL by alternating metric-learned
refinement and embedding updates \citep{chen2020idgl}, and NodeFormer and DGM by
making structure differentiable and scalable
\citep{wu2022nodeformer,kazi2023dgm}. Self-supervised variants remove the label
dependence \citep{liu2022sublime}, and a robustness-oriented branch purifies an
observed adjacency against noise or attack
\citep{jin2020prognn,zhang2020gnnguard}. Masked graph autoencoding forms a
parallel line in which structure itself is the pretraining signal, from VGAE
\citep{kipf2016vgae} through GraphMAE and MaskGAE
\citep{hou2022graphmae,li2023maskgae}. Surveys map this space
\citep{zhu2021gslsurvey}.

None of this family solves our task, for four separable reasons. (i) The
robustness branch presupposes a given graph containing the query nodes and
refines it; this is our post-hoc denoising baseline, not a generator. (ii) The
latent-adjacency branch optimizes structure indexed by node identity over one
closed node set, and that structure is never recomputed from frozen features for
a node the optimization never saw. (iii) The downstream task is almost always
node classification, so the generated structure is validated as a message-passing
substrate and never as evidence for an $\mathrm{edge}(u,v)$ decision. (iv) The
masked-autoencoding branch masks edges of nodes already inside the training
adjacency, which is transductive link completion rather than induction to unseen
nodes.

\paragraph{Generated structure as intermediate context.}
Our scaffold generator draws on graph generation for machinery, not for the task.
Autoregressive generators \citep{you2018graphrnn} and discrete diffusion models
\citep{vignac2023digress} supply conditional sparse-adjacency construction, and
\citet{chanpuriya2021edge} give the formal reason a generator is needed at all
rather than a factorized score: any edge-independent model provably cannot
achieve realistic triangle and clustering density without approaching
memorization, so clustering structure cannot emerge from independent pairwise
probabilities. Neural relational inference is the cleanest cross-domain statement
of latent structure inferred to serve a downstream predictive task
\citep{kipf2018nri}. Outside graphs, the pattern of a generative module supplying
intermediate context to a discriminative decision is well established:
hallucinated examples for low-shot recognition \citep{wang2018lowshot}, generated
features for zero-shot classification \citep{xian2018fgn,xian2019fvaegan}, and
CVAE-generated neighbor \emph{features} conditioned on a center node's own
features, resampled each iteration and never themselves scored
\citep{liu2022localaug}. We instantiate that pattern for topology rather than
features.

Two contrasts sharpen the boundary. Latent graph diffusion collapses
classification into conditional generation by diffusing toward the label
\citep{zhou2024latentgraphdiffusion}, the opposite of our design, in which the
generative step is strictly upstream of an unchanged binary decision. FLEX is the
closest published mechanism: it generates a counterfactual subgraph conditioned
on a queried link to improve out-of-distribution link prediction
\citep{revolinsky2025flex}. It differs on two axes that matter---its generated
subgraph is a \emph{training-time} augmentation rather than inference-time
per-query context, and its nodes remain exposed to observed graph structure
rather than being feature-only and unseen. Finally, the graph generation
evaluation literature grounds our reporting rules: per-statistic MMD over degree,
clustering, and orbit descriptors is the standard realism battery, but
\citet{thompson2022evaluation} show it is measurably blind to mode collapse and
sensitive to unstandardized kernel bandwidth, and \citet{obray2022evaluation}
formalize expressivity, robustness, and efficiency as the desiderata any such
metric must meet. We therefore report GS, RD, and the three MMD ratios separately
and never aggregate them into a single score.
